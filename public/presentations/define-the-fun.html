Define the Fun
A play on "find the fun." Find it, define it, then carry the part that lasts.
What makes something fun, which fun lasts, and how to ride it.

Start with the map

There are at least seven kinds of fun.

MasteryDiscoveryImmersion BelongingExpressionThrill ComfortMeaning

Drawn from a century of research: Bartle, Caillois, Lazzaro, Koster, the MDA framework, and Quantic Foundry's data on 140,000 players. A real map. But a map is flat.

The turn

The question isn't what's fun. It's which fun lasts.

Pleasure feels good now and asks nothing of you. Purpose costs something and stays with you afterward. Every kind of fun can be lived shallow or deep. The rest of this is one answer to what moves it from one to the other, in three beliefs.

Belief 01 · the edge of engagement

Fun lives on the edge of order and chaos.

Between what you already know and what you don't yet. Too far into the known and you are bored. Too far into the unknown and you are overwhelmed. The narrow band between is where attention sharpens and you actually learn.

In the research

Flow sits in a channel between boredom and anxiety. Koster: fun is the brain's reward for learning a pattern. The Goldilocks effect: infants attend to events of intermediate complexity, ignoring the too-simple and the too-complex. A century of separate research keeps rediscovering the same border.

One picture

The edge is a channel you ride, not a place you arrive.

The same picture works twice. Read the diagonal as knowledge and it explains engagement. Read it as responsibility and it explains purpose. Sit below it and you stagnate; rise above it and you break; ride it and you grow, which pushes the whole channel outward.

Belief 02 · the edge of meaning

Purpose comes from taking on as much responsibility as you can without breaking.

Too little and nothing rests on you, so nothing matters. Too much and you break. Purpose is the band between, where the weight is real but bearable. The same edge as belief one, measured in weight instead of knowledge.

In the research

Frankl found meaning through responsibility, and wanted a Statue of Responsibility to stand opposite the Statue of Liberty. Aristotle's eudaimonia is flourishing through effort, not pleasure. Taleb's antifragility is the exact meaning of "without breaking": stress strengthens you, up to a dose. All verified, including the Frankl quote.

Belief 03 · the escalating wave

You can't hold the edge forever. You ride it as a wave.

Sit pinned at your limit and you fray: fatigue, then burnout, then breaking. Challenge has to rise and fall. Spike at a boss, drop into a valley to recover and consolidate, then come back to a higher floor. The whole wave climbs up and to the right.

In the research

Schell's interest curve is exactly this: peaks and valleys, trending up to a climax. Supercompensation: a stress plus recovery leaves your baseline higher; constant stress with no recovery just overtrains you. Selye: skip recovery and you run through resistance into exhaustion. The valleys are not filler. They are how you climb without breaking.

One picture

Spike at the boss. Recover in the valley. Return stronger.

Each valley sits above the last; each crest clears a bar the previous you could not. The breaking point rises with you, so the wave climbs without ever touching it, as long as the valleys are real.

The wave is personal

How tall the peaks, how deep the valleys: that's a dial.

Dark Souls
High amplitude

Tall, constant peaks; short valleys. For high sensation-seekers who want to live near the edge.

World of Warcraft
Rare towering crest

Long, gentle valleys (grinding), then an occasional huge peak (a hard raid). Mass appeal.

Candy Crush · TikTok
Near-flat wind-down

Almost no peaks, little climb. For people who, right now, do not want to be challenged at all.

It tracks a real, stable trait: sensation seeking. Match your audience's amplitude appetite, or better, let players tune it themselves.

Proof in play

Ticket to Ride is the whole thesis in a box.

Commit to too few destination tickets and you cannot score enough to win. Commit to too many and you fail them for a penalty. The entire game is one question: how much can I take on without breaking? Twenty minutes, no narrative, the edge of responsibility made into a turn.

The deep end

11 bit built a studio on handing you weight.

Frostpunk, This War of Mine, The Alters. You are the law of the last city, or the conscience of people in a siege. Not fun in the hedonic sense. Meaningful, because the responsibility is genuinely yours. Proof that the eudaimonic edge is a market, not just a virtue.

The control group

TikTok proves it by doing the opposite.

It pins you to the engagement edge better than anything ever built, and asks nothing of you: no weight, no peaks, no climb. Total engagement that never becomes purpose, which is why an hour on it evaporates. Engagement without responsibility is pleasure that never converts. Retention is not meaning.

The tool

Every kind of fun has a purpose setting. The dial is responsibility.

Mastery
"I got good" → people rely on the standard I hold
Belonging
"fun with people" → they are counting on me, and I show up
Expression
"I made a thing" → I own what I put into the world
Thrill
"what a rush" → I owned the risk I chose, win or lose
Comfort
the valley of the wave: the recovery that makes the next peak survivable
Where I'm taking this

I'd rather build the thing that asks something of you.

Chosen, escalating, survivable responsibility, paced as a wave, so play becomes practice for a meaningful life instead of a substitute for one. Most of the industry is flattening the wave to maximize time-on-screen. I want to go the other way.

What it adds up to

Define the fun. Then build the kind that asks something of you, and gives you room to come back stronger.

Three beliefs, one model: ride the edge of order and chaos, carry as much as you can without breaking, and climb it as a wave.
